# 2. Problem: The system hallucinates clauses that do not exist

## What the system says

Your summary states:

> Obligation to maintain confidentiality of proprietary information

## What the document contains

There is no confidentiality clause in the agreement.

There is nothing about:

* confidential information
* proprietary information
* disclosure
* non-disclosure
* secrecy
* confidential data

The agreement moves from identifying the parties and premises into financial/payment information and other administrative details. 

## What is technically happening

Your summarization model is probably doing something like:

```text
Rental Agreement
      ↓
LLM recognizes "contract"
      ↓
LLM recalls common contract concepts
      ↓
Confidentiality is common in contracts
      ↓
LLM includes confidentiality in summary
```

This is called **template hallucination** or **schema completion hallucination**.

The LLM knows what a "typical agreement" may contain and fills missing sections using its learned expectations.

### Why this is dangerous in legal AI

A normal summarizer making this mistake is bad.

A legal document analyzer making this mistake is much worse because the user may believe:

> "I have a confidentiality obligation."

when **they may not have one under that agreement**.

## The critical mistake

The system is converting **external legal reasoning into a document clause**.

These are fundamentally different things.

### Contract evidence

```text

```

This would be a contract clause.

### External legal information


This would be external legal reasoning.

Your system appears to have merged them and presented the result as:

> "The agreement says your termination notice is 30 days."

That is a **source attribution failure**.

# 6. Problem: The system cannot properly handle absence

This is probably the **single most important technical problem**.

Your system appears designed to always produce an answer.

But in document analysis, there are at least four possible outcomes:

### Outcome 1 — Clause found

```text
Question:
What is the monthly rent?

Evidence:
₹42,000

Answer:
₹42,000
```

### Outcome 2 — Clause found but ambiguous

```text
Question:
Can I terminate early?

Evidence:
Termination clause exists but wording is unclear.

Answer:
Explain ambiguity.
```

### Outcome 3 — Clause not found

```text
Question:
What is my notice period?

Evidence:
No termination clause.

Answer:
The agreement does not specify a notice period.
```

### Outcome 4 — Document is silent, but external law may apply

```text
Question:
What happens if I don't pay rent?

Contract:
No consequence clause.

Law:
External legal rules may govern the situation.

Answer:
Clearly separate contract from law.
```

Your current system seems to skip Outcome 3.

Instead:

```text
No evidence found
      ↓
LLM still required to answer
      ↓
LLM uses general legal knowledge
      ↓
Hallucinated contractual answer
```

You need an explicit:

# `NO_EVIDENCE_FOUND`

state.

# My strongest recommendation

If you fix only **three things**, fix these.

## 1. Evidence Gate

The model must not make a document-specific claim unless evidence exists.

```text
Claim proposed
      ↓
Is supporting document span available?
      │
   ┌──┴──┐
  YES    NO
   │      │
Answer    "Not specified in the document"
```

---

## 2. Separate three answer modes

```text
┌─────────────────────────────┐
│ CONTRACT EVIDENCE           │
│ "The agreement states..."   │
└─────────────────────────────┘

┌─────────────────────────────┐
│ CONTRACT SILENCE            │
│ "The agreement does not     │
│ specify..."                 │
└─────────────────────────────┘

┌─────────────────────────────┐
│ EXTERNAL LEGAL INFORMATION  │
│ "Separately, applicable law │
│ may provide..."             │
└─────────────────────────────┘
```

---

## 3. Never index generated summaries as primary legal evidence

Your retrieval priority should be:

```text
Priority 1 → Original clause text
Priority 2 → Extracted structured facts
Priority 3 → Clause metadata
Priority 4 → AI-generated summary
```

And ideally:

> **AI-generated summaries should never be used as authoritative evidence for chatbot answers.**

They can help navigation, but the final answer must always be verified against the original clause.

---

## Final verdict

**Your project concept is strong, but currently the legal reasoning layer is overconfident and insufficiently grounded.**

The most dangerous characteristic is not that it says *"I don't know."* The dangerous characteristic is that **when the document does not contain an answer, your system still produces a confident legal answer with statutes and citations**.

For a legal AI system, your ideal behaviour should be:

> **"I found this."**

> **"I did not find this in the agreement."**

> **"This is not in the agreement, but external law may be relevant."**

Those three distinctions should become the foundation of both your **5-tier risk matrix and your chatbot architecture**.
