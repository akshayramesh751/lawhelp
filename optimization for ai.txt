## The important part: Cache key design

Do **not** use:

```text id="yp4pjq"
hash(clauseText)
```

Instead:

```text id="c1p4cc"
cacheKey = SHA-256(
    normalizedClauseText
    + relevantDocumentContextHash
    + clauseType
    + documentDomain
    + jurisdiction
    + retrievedAuthorityHash
    + legalKnowledgeVersion
    + promptVersion
)
```

### Why `relevantDocumentContextHash`?

Because the same clause can have different meanings in different documents.

Example:

```text id="8f66u7"
Document A:

"Notice period is 30 days."

Context:
Both employer and employee follow 30 days.
```

vs:

```text id="ij2ao5"
Document B:

"Notice period is 30 days."

Context:
Employer can terminate immediately.
Employee must provide 30 days.
```

Same clause text:

```text id="y4sp8z"
"Notice period is 30 days."
```

But:

```text id="slywhp"
Different Context Hash
```

Therefore:

```text id="dxohz6"
Different Cache Key
```

Therefore:

```text id="7e9omz"
No accidental reuse
```

---

## What should `relevantDocumentContext` contain?

Only information that affects the clause.

For example:

```text id="w27q3y"
• Referenced definitions
• Previous/next related clauses
• Governing law clause
• Party roles
• Cross-referenced sections
```

You don't necessarily need the entire document.

This is important because including the entire document hash would make the cache extremely strict and greatly reduce cache hits.
